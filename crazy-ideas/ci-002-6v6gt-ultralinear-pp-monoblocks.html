6V6GT Ultralinear Push-Pull Monoblocks

A source-grounded review of A. C. Wyatt’s compact, externally powered 6V6GT monoblock and a feasibility screen for the RIOT 10″ Bianco midrange.

RESEARCH — NOT APPROVED FOR BUILD

Source and scope

Primary source: A 6V6 Ultra Linear Push-Pull Mono-bloc Amplifier, A. C. Wyatt BSc FIP3, The Valve Museum / R-Type, 24 November 2021. The construction and test results below are the author’s report. RIOT application estimates and modifications are identified separately.

The article adapts a Dynaco-derived Chinese kit board into two single-channel amplifiers. The source article contains the original schematic and build photographs; this record links to it rather than redrawing unverified component values.

Published circuit and operating arrangement

Output stageOne push-pull pair of 6V6GT beam tetrodes per monoblock, with ultralinear screen-grid drive; author describes the circuit as Dynaco-derived.
Driver / feedbackThe kit’s double-triode stage is retained. The negative-feedback path returns from the transformer’s 16 Ω tap. During initial fault-finding, the feedback input had to be grounded; the output-transformer primary leads then had to be reversed to correct positive feedback.
Output transformerHammond 1608A. The author grounds the 16 Ω feedback tap, uses the 8 Ω tap for the speaker, and cuts/insulates the unused 4 Ω tap.
HT255 V for the reported 5 W test, chosen below the Dynaco circuit’s 300–325 V rail to avoid pursuing maximum output. The author later reports 6.6 W after raising HT to 300 V.
Heaters6.3 V DC from a DC-DC step-down converter; current limit set to 1.5 A to limit heater inrush. The article does not provide heater ripple or regulation measurements.
External input supplySeparate 19.5 V DC, at least 3.3 A per monoblock. The author measured 1.4 A steady input current; smaller laptop supplies tripped on capacitor-charging surge.
Physical layoutCompact die-cast enclosure, 187 × 119 × 56 mm, with the output transformer and valve board arranged for top-side access. No mains AC enters the amplifier enclosure; separate external supplies are used for the two channels.

Construction and integration issues reported by the author

  • The stereo kit board was cut down the centre to make two monoblocks. Exposed cut copper had to be kept clear of the metal case; nylon standoffs provided spacing and insulation.
  • Precise valve-holder and transformer holes required a paper template and a 27 mm Q-Max-style cutter. The enclosure’s centre pillar also needed filing to clear the board supports.
  • The 250 Ω, 2 W cathode resistor was assembled from three 750 Ω, 1 W resistors in parallel; spacing was added around the resistor bodies.
  • A chassis-mounted 2.1 mm DC jack tied converter input ground to the chassis and introduced audible buzz, likely through a ground loop carrying converter switching noise. Adding 1,500 μF at the converter input only appeared to help temporarily.
  • The author removed that jack, kept the 19.5 V input conductors separate from output ground, and used a three-pin GX-12 connector. Separate laptop supplies were used per channel to avoid earth-loop hum.
  • The HT converter was mounted on the enclosure base/lid and reportedly ran cool. Capacitor mounting, lead insulation, clearances and the exposed converter underside all needed mechanical attention.

Practical point: the converter grounding and start-up surge were real build issues in the article. They matter if this is ever adapted to RIOT; the author’s fix depends on the exact converter, chassis and connector wiring.

RIOT feasibility: 10″ Bianco midrange

Potential role: one 6V6GT monoblock per speaker, driven from the existing active crossover into the SB Audience Bianco 10MW150 band (approximately 300 Hz to 1.8–1.9 kHz). This is an amplifier alternative for investigation only; it does not change the current system or any amplifier baseline.

The project record uses approximately 100 dB sensitivity for the 10MW150. As a rough screening calculation, assuming 100 dB SPL at 1 W/1 m and ideal free-field inverse-distance loss, 5 W would produce about 107 dB at 1 m and 98.4 dB at 2.7 m for one speaker. At the author’s 6.6 W / 300 V result, the same estimate is about 108.2 dB at 1 m and 99.6 dB at 2.7 m. These are calculations from the nominal sensitivity and author’s power figures, not RIOT measurements; room contribution, compression, actual sensitivity across the band, crossover gain and listening peaks are unknown. The result suggests limited margin against RIOT’s occasional 100 dB peak target at the seat unless the real conditions provide additional headroom.

Potential fit

  • The 10MW150’s high nominal sensitivity makes a 5–6.6 W amplifier plausible for ordinary listening levels.
  • One monoblock per channel suits the existing stereo active-crossover layout.
  • Ultralinear 6V6 push-pull is a concrete, published circuit with a measured 8 Ω output result.

Unknowns before any build decision

  • Input sensitivity, gain, input impedance and noise relative to the existing crossover output.
  • Frequency response, distortion spectrum, clipping onset, output impedance and stability over 300 Hz–1.9 kHz.
  • Whether Hammond 1608A performance and feedback remain suitable with this band, load and the 10MW150 impedance curve.
  • Two-channel gain matching, turn-on sequence, hum, heat and sustained midband power in the RIOT enclosure and room.

Speculative RIOT modification: Class A operation

RIOT proposal only · not in the source article

Running the push-pull output pair in Class A is an unverified idea, not a setting established by the published build. The article reports 31 mA for its matched valves and gives no Class A test, idle-dissipation analysis or modified transformer/power-supply limits. A Class A arrangement would require a new operating-point design and checks of plate and screen dissipation, output-transformer DC balance, cathode network power, HT-converter current, heat and maximum clean output. The author’s 5 W / 6.6 W results cannot be carried over to that modification.

Classification remains: RESEARCH — NOT APPROVED FOR BUILD. Do not change the 10″ amplifier baseline, crossover gain or system wiring on the strength of this concept record.

Decision gates if promoted to a real comparison

  1. Identify the exact published schematic and kit-board revision from the article, then check the Hammond 1608A data and winding connections against it.
  2. Model the 10MW150 impedance and desired 300 Hz–1.9 kHz band against the transformer load and feedback arrangement.
  3. Measure an assembled example into resistive and representative reactive loads: gain, frequency response, noise, THD versus output, clipping and thermal behaviour.
  4. Check source/crossover compatibility and match left/right gain before any driver connection.
  5. Compare at matched listening level with the current mid amplifier, Ciuffoli and a specified KT66 design. Keep the Class A variant as a separate experiment.
