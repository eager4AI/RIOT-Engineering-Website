12 inch bass cabinets

  • The current archive describes the cabinets as bass-reflex/Helmholtz systems, not transmission lines.

  • Earlier design record: approximately 72 L chamber with two front slots, each approximately 444 x 25 x 180 mm; total vent area approximately 222 cm2.

  • Earlier tuning estimates range approximately 48-53 Hz depending on the design revision.

  • The intended behaviour is a low-stress, high-headroom kick/bass engine rather than a small woofer driven near its limits.

  • Cabinet photographs, exact as-built dimensions and final tuning measurement remain to be attached.

10 inch Bianco cabinets

  • Driver: SB Audience Bianco 10MW150.

  • Target role: live-scale body and violence through the midrange, approximately 300 Hz to 1.8-1.9 kHz.

  • Recorded geometry for the waveguide-in-cabinet trial: mouth approximately 480 mm, throat-side geometry approximately 210 mm, depth approximately 212 mm, R500 flare.

  • Rear chamber record: approximately 150 x 442 x 290 mm; coil-to-back clearance approximately 87 mm.

  • Cabinet completion and final acoustic measurement remain open.

CD44-PK horns

  • Compression driver: SB Audience CD44-PK, 1 inch CD.

  • Custom horn is intended for approximately 1.9 kHz upward.

  • IWATA-600 and IWATA-300 references were acquired; an oblate-spheroid/OS waveguide alternative was also considered.

  • Horn depth is approximately 220 mm in the system record.

  • Home 3D printing is part of the construction plan; final printed geometry and acoustic verification remain open.

Listening evidence

  • The project target is live real-fi: physical impact, immediate attack, believable scale and effortless dynamics rather than audiophile politeness.

  • Recorded listening reports include strong snare attack without added decay, compelling tribal-percussion timing and bodily kicks that do not obscure the drum solo.

  • Earlier listening references include Private Investigations, Time, Angel, Chocolate Chip Trip, Keith Don’t Go and Cosmic Girl.

Where to resume

Finish the physical 10 inch cabinets and horn printing, then run the fixed-position REW set from the Room Acoustics dossier. Do not interpret a speaker response without recording the amplifier, crossover and source chain used for that capture.

Evidence status

System bands and current amplifier assignments are explicit project facts. Some cabinet dimensions, port dimensions, tuning values and driver data belong to different design revisions; the final as-built measurements must be tagged by revision before being treated as authoritative.

Fault-isolation chronology

  • Strong radio-like interference at left 10-inch mid; right mid and left LF/HF quiet.
  • Left GC reportedly normal before onset; no deliberate GC circuit modification.
  • Removing input XLR silenced driver.
  • Swapping incoming left/right XLR cables at amplifier side left fault with LEFT amplifier/speaker.
  • Crossover mains off/unplugged did not clear fault.
  • Disturbance also occurred with far cable end floating.
  • HOT-to-GND short at left amplifier XLR input worsened oscillation.

CONFIRMED versus LIKELY

CONFIRMED as reported: the experiments localize the fault to the left Gainclone amplifier assembly rather than DAC, crossover or incoming cable. Shorted-input HF activity and associated rail sag reproduce on the left; the right reference is essentially flat under the same condition.

LIKELY, NOT PROVEN: a failed LM3886 IC is the prime suspect, given the simple topology, right-channel control and earlier project history of a failed LM3886 with significant DC output. Solder, PCB grounding, feedback capacitor and other passive failures are not definitively excluded. Do not label the IC failure VERIFIED.

Decisions and PENDING repair validation

Stop repeated non-diagnostic in-circuit resistance tests. No spare LM3886 is on hand. Procure a replacement with the package/mounting variant matched to the fitted device; T versus TF insulation requirements differ. Do not operate the faulty amp into a speaker or prolong oscillation.

PENDING: replacement procurement, actual component substitution, successful retest and actual root-cause confirmation. No repair is completed. Use speaker-disconnected, current-limited first power-up; verify mounting insulation/shorts, DC offset, shorted-input output waveform, supply rails and thermal behaviour. If replacing the LM3886 does not cure the fault, reopen PCB/passive investigation.

Missing evidence: source scope photos were shown in the original conversation but their original bytes are not attached to issue #2. Source transfer is requested when available; no scope image, exact oscillation frequency, calibration or completed repair is claimed. Full issue snapshot and intake administration remain private.
